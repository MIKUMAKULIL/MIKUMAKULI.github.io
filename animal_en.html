🟢 Species No. 1: Transparent Crane / Chupocabricus Ohevanchik (Chupocabricus ohevanchik / Lat. "Completely Out of Hand")

Habitat Range: Shaded underplots of Western Europe, abandoned livestock farms, and damp lowlands of the polders in Delft.

Anatomical and Biophysical Characteristics:
This exogenous parasite represents a highly specialized form of ectoparasitic acariform mite, reaching deterministic dimensions of up to 3 centimeters. The subject's body possesses absolute transparency; however, epidermal chromatophores allow it to mimic the pigmentation of the host's skin tissue, operating via chameleon-like systemic principles.

The parasite's morphology is thoroughly anomalous: it is equipped with **47 differentiated micro-limbs**. Within each individual leg runs an autonomous nerve trunk and a terminal oral orifice, precisely adapted for the simultaneous absorption of capillary blood and the host's lipid (fat) layer. On the dorsal side, seven rudimentary black eyes are situated. Reproduction occurs via accelerated vegetative budding directly upon the victim's epithelium. Extinction (destruction) of the imago is achieved exclusively through targeted thermal application (burning out).

"From the secret field journal of M. Shakespeare: I strongly warn my colleagues! It is categorically forbidden to forcefully rip the Ohevanchik off the skin! If this methodological error is committed, the 47 micro-limbs will remain deeply embedded within the dermis, triggering extensive necrosis. The only valid extirpation protocol is strictly surgical. One must carefully and sequentially amputate each of the forty-seven legs from the main syndosomatose body, before extracting the capsule itself (it does not regenerate post-extraction). The legs remaining in the skin will eventually dessicate and evacuate the organism naturally over time.

Personal Note: Good Heavens, how I despise them. An existential horror and deep revulsion overcome me whenever I am forced to extract these tiny, twitching legs with a pair of tweezers... Each one emits a barely audible squeak when severed. Thank science, during field trials I discovered their critical vulnerability to phytoncides: Ohevanchiks plunge into immediate paralytic shock upon direct contact with citrus extracts, essential oils, and, most surprisingly, French perfume with a concentrated scent of lavender."

🔴 Species No. 2: Fractal Chernobyl Vole / Madagascan Chepushilik (Chepushilikus madagascariensis)

Habitat Range: Endemic tropical rainforests of Madagascar, isolated plateaus, and coastal strips.

Eco-Informational Phenomenon and Biomechanics:
This subject represents the greatest and most inexplicable faunal paradox of the 20th century, inducing permanent cognitive dissonance and sheer panic among classical zoologists. Morphologically, the individual exhibits feline traits, yet it is uniquely weaponized with **one central compound facet eye, ten hollow horny outgrowths, and eight symmetrically distributed limbs**.

The primary anomaly of the Chepushilik lies within its mode of propulsion. It utilizes so-called dynamic spherical locomotion—meaning it rolls itself into a perfect ring and tumbles across surfaces like a wheel. Due to the synchronized propulsive impulse of all eight legs, this species is officially recognized as **the fastest mammal on planet Earth**, hitting unprecedented speeds of up to 300 km/h on flat terrain. Interdisciplinary commissions are absolutely terrified by its versatility: the Chepushilik walks, climbs vertical trees, swims in saltwater with equal efficiency, and is capable of short gliding flights due to the aerodynamic structure of its horns.

Its diet consists exclusively of rare endemic berries, though individuals exhibit a bizarre behavioral deviation—an irresistible urge to tactually bite human footwear (primarily the canvas field boots of expedition staff).

"From Chapter IV of the Monograph: My peers from the Paris Academy of Sciences locked themselves in their laboratory and declared the Chepushilik an 'impossible bio-object that violates the laws of thermodynamics and evolution.' But I categorically disagree with their academic snobbery! This is the most charming creature. Upon a human's approach, the Chepushilik generates a low-frequency vibration (intense purring), expressing deep social interest. Contact with humans is nothing but a game to them.

Their offspring ('kittens') trigger special scientific delight. During early ontogenesis, they cannot yet control their braking vector. Before my very eyes, a litter of tiny Chepushiliks purred their way up to 150 km/h on a polder, failed to brake in front of my tent, went airborne, got their horns stuck in an oak canopy, and just kept rolling along the branches from there, trying to dive-bomb my boots. Official science can stay in terror all it wants, but I consider them the pinnacle of biospheric creation."

🔵 Species No. 3: Delft Glow-in-the-Dark Finch (Fringilla lucifuga)

Habitat Range: Technogenic wasteland sectors, chemical industrial zones of Rotterdam, and urbanized drainage systems of Delft.

Ecological Marker and Mutation:
This passerine bio-indicator serves as living evidence of severe environmental shifts in Western Europe. Under the heavy pressure of synthetic chemical discharges and heavy metal accumulation at the turn of the late 20th century, the species underwent a rapid mutagenic adaptation. Its plumage contains unique phosphorus-binding enzymes that metabolize industrial waste, causing the bird's breast and wing coverts to emit a distinct, continuous bioluminescent glow in dark or foggy environments. Local authorities tried to classify reports of "glowing swarms" to prevent real estate panic, but the bird's adaptation allowed it to successfully hunt nocturnal insects near industrial smoke stacks.

"From the Appendices to the Monograph: When I first captured a specimen of Fringilla lucifuga behind an old chemical refinery in Delft, it literally illuminated my entire field desk like a 40-watt bulb. The municipal council called it a 'unfortunate statistical deviation,' but I see it as nature's direct retaliation against toxic greed. The bird doesn't suffer from the pollution; it harvests it, turning industrial waste into a glowing warning sign for humanity. It is an absolute triumph of tactical ecosystem survival!"
